Title: Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks
Authors: Patrick Lewis, Ethan Perez, Aleksandra Piktus, Fabio Petroni
Subject: Computation and Language; Artificial Intelligence; Machine Learning

Abstract
Large pre-trained language models store substantial amounts of factual knowledge in their parameters, yet they suffer from hallucinations, opacity, and out-of-date information. We explore general-purpose fine-tuning recipes for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)—models which combine pre-trained parametric memory with non-parametric external memory accessed through dense neural retrieval. We evaluate RAG models on knowledge-intensive benchmarks including Natural Questions, WebQuestions, and CuratedTREC. Our results show that RAG architectures establish state-of-the-art results on open-domain question answering, generating more specific, diverse, and factual language than parametric-only sequence-to-sequence models. We also observe that dense retrieval does not universally outperform sparse lexical search across all domains: sparse BM25 remains superior for rare technical tokens and exact numeric codes.

1. Introduction
Parametric models encode knowledge implicitly in model weights, which makes updating or verifying facts difficult without full re-training. In contrast, non-parametric retrieval architectures allow models to dynamically consult external document stores. A critical question in research retrieval is whether dense embeddings universally supersede sparse lexical indexing (such as BM25).

2. Dense Retrieval vs. Sparse Lexical Search
In our comparative evaluation between dense passage retrieval (DPR) and BM25:
Dense retrieval demonstrates superior semantic generalization on in-domain academic queries, successfully retrieving paraphrased formulations of concepts.
However, contrary to common assumptions that dense vectors are universally superior, BM25 outperforms dense vectors on rare technical identifiers, acronyms, and out-of-distribution codes.
When tested on datasets containing exact numerical thresholds or software API function signatures, BM25 achieves a Recall@5 of 84.1%, whereas pure dense retrieval drops to 71.3%.
Therefore, we observe that hybrid retrieval combining dense semantic embeddings with BM25 lexical ranking achieves the most robust overall recall across heterogeneous benchmarks.

3. Faithfulness and Latency
Our experiments show that grounding generation with non-parametric retrieval reduces hallucination rates by 42% compared to purely parametric T5 and GPT baselines.
Furthermore, vector search provides millisecond-level approximate nearest neighbor lookup with minimal graph overhead, maintaining sub-40ms latency on multi-million passage indexes.
However, our analysis indicates that retrieval models can still hallucinate when provided with ambiguous or irrelevant retrieved contexts, highlighting the necessity of strict citation verification mechanisms.
